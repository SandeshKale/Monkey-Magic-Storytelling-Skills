# 03 Conflict

A situation becomes dramatic because of conflict. Conflict is the problem a character must solve to reach a goal.

## Pieces

- Goal — defeat the British at cricket, save the princess, cross India without spending the reserve.
- Antagonist — person, institution, parent, weather, body, object, self.
- Stakes — what breaks if they fail. Triple tax. Public shame. Empty wallet.
- Multiple conflicts — one major plus several mid-size. Flat stories have only the headline goal.

The stronger the antagonist, the greater the hero. Ravana is a scholar with a golden city. That is why Rama's victory lands. A weak obstacle makes a weak video.

## Stacking (Lagaan pattern, reusable)

1. Headline conflict — win the match or pay triple tax.
2. Own people do not believe the protagonist.
3. Nobody has the skill the goal requires.
4. Someone inside the team betrays.
5. Body or weather turns against them.

Stack to the runtime tier in the canonical numbers table in SKILL.md (long 3–6, mid 2–3, Short 1), not one.

These are patterns, not events. Use only conflicts that occurred or that the creator will really face. Do not invent a betrayer or a doubter.

## Object and situation antagonists

Parents who block a singing dream are an antagonistic force. An unfit body walking into MMA is an antagonistic force. A wall seat on a 4-day train is an antagonistic force. Name it. If the antagonist is a real private person, follow the people rules in safety-and-consent and prefer the role or system over the name.

## Script rule

Write

`Goal —`
`If they fail —`
`Antagonist (named) —`
`Conflict 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 —`

If "if they fail" is "I get fewer views," the stakes are fake. Find a human stake or drop the video.
