# 01 What is a story

Stories are more durable than events. They change what a culture believes. A YouTube video that does not contain a story is an anecdote with lighting.

## Test

An incident is a story only if the character does not leave with the same mindset they arrived with.

Physical surroundings may also change. That is optional. Mindset change is not.

## Worked contrast (teaching pattern, do not copy facts)

Not a story — "I rode my bike to the studio and saw a crash." That is news.

Story — "I loved riding fast. I saw a rider without a helmet go through a windscreen. I called the ambulance. After that my own bike would not go past 40, and the first thing I did in the studio was order a helmet." The belief changed.

Not a story — "I did a comedy set. People clapped."

Story — "I said anything for a laugh. A couple stormed the stage. Police called me back at midnight. I apologized in public. I now think before I aim a joke." Mindset changed.

Pitch test from the course — Breaking Bad as "a chemistry teacher who becomes Scarface." The one-liner is the change.

## YouTube implication

Most daily vlogs fail because they are routines. Waking, water, work, repeat. No change, no drama, no reason to stay.

When a user brings a day-in-the-life, hunt for the moment their belief moved. If none exists, do not invent one. Either pick a different day or switch to an idea-based video that still contains a small honest change.

## Script rule

Before writing dialogue, write

`Start belief —`
`Event —`
`End belief —`

If start and end match, it is not a story yet.
