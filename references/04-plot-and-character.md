# 04 Plot and character

Plot is the physical activity around the character. Character is who the audience attaches to. Plot brilliance without character dies after one season. Character with a modest plot can run for years.

## Plot

Can be quiet (a NASA scientist comes home for his nanny and stays to wire a village) or loud (a letter arrives from a magic school). Both are plot. Neither is enough.

Money Heist season one is a strong plot. Later seasons lose people when characters stop being memorable. Breaking Bad and Better Call Saul hold because the people hold.

## Character on YouTube

People click the next video for the person. MrBeast-scale production copied by a faceless studio underperforms because the audience did not walk eleven years with that studio. MKBHD's robots matter because viewers watched the bedroom-to-warehouse climb.

The channel and creator examples here are the instructor's illustrations, not verified facts. Do not repeat them as facts in a user's script.

The creator is the protagonist. State the character's public goal the way MrBeast does ("best YouTube videos on earth") so the audience can root for progress.

## 70 / 30

Focus 70 percent of the writing on the character (beliefs, flaws, history, present discomfort) and 30 percent on the idea (the train, the gadget, the challenge). TV is usually character-driven. Film can be plot-driven. YouTube behaves like TV that lives in someone's pocket.

## Object as character

A product can be the character if you put it in the wrong place. iPhone in a dustbin. Car that cannot hold the luggage it was sold for. The mismatch does the drama. The creator still has to be a person in the frame or on the voice.

## Script rule

If you can swap the creator for any other YouTuber and the script still works, character is too thin. Add one true particular (a belief, a scar, a running goal) that only this person has.
