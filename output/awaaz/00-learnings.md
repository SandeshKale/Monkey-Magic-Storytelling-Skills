## Rules I took from the two real sources
1. **Beat Engine (Filmustage):** Hook, Friction, Spike, Button.
   - **Hook (first seconds):** an opening jolt. "If a stranger needs backstory to understand what they are seeing, you are not there yet."
   - **Friction:** real conflict in the frame, between people under pressure.
   - **Spike:** the biggest single jolt. **Mute test:** if you mute the audio and the spike disappears, it was not a spike.
   - **Button (last 5 to 10 seconds):** cut on the **question, not the answer**. For a 60 to 90 second episode the freeze should land around 55 to 58 seconds.
2. **Timestamp skeleton before dialogue.** Draft the beats by seconds first, then write the lines.
3. **Characters:** 2 to 3 central characters per episode. Ensembles confuse short episodes.
4. **The frame is faces, hands and intimacy.** Two people at a kitchen table beats a wide stadium shot. Long physical sequences do not translate to 9:16.
5. **Dialogue economy:** every line advances conflict or reveals character under pressure. No small talk, no exposition, no monologues. Characters act on feelings, not explain them.
6. **A simple engine (InkTip):** one clear want, a fresh obstacle every episode, one open question. Drop us into the middle of the conflict in the first five seconds.
7. **Cliffhanger types that work:** a revelation, a reversal, a deadline, an intrusion.
8. **Series arc:** plan 60 to 100 episodes up front in four phases (establish, escalate, dark middle, resolve).

## Constraints specific to Grok that shaped the script
- **5-second clips** (12 per episode). One shot each, one action, one or two short lines.
- **Lip-sync is hard with two voices in one shot,** so the cloned voice is always heard through a phone speaker, with no lips on screen to match it.
- **Two speakers per clip at most, one man and one woman,** so Grok gives them different voices.
- **Text is added in post** (captions, UPI screens, call banners).
